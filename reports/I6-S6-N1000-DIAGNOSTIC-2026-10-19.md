## Mechanism and limits

For a Gaussian resample, `runner.R` computes the exact comparison probability p, then simulates d as `Binomial(200000,p)/200000`. This has the same distribution as the mean of 200,000 independent success indicators from its Gaussian `pmed_scores()` comparison. Near s6's null, the saved median p gives mean p(1-p) = 0.2499796, so one bootstrap estimate has Monte Carlo SD about sqrt(0.25/200000) = 0.001118. The exact N=1000 mean percentile width implies an approximate bootstrap SD of 0.017852854/(2 z_0.975) = 0.004554 if its central distribution is normal. Adding those variances predicts a width ratio of 1.029691, close to the observed 1.029588. The same approximation tracks all five s6 sample sizes in the table as the exact bootstrap distribution narrows with N. I4's independent run also missed s6/N=1000 (coverage difference +0.0085, width ratio about 1.030).

This normal-spread calculation is explanatory, not a proof of the exact endpoint distribution. The saved outputs contain interval endpoints rather than all 2,000 bootstrap values per outer replication, so this review cannot attribute every endpoint change to a particular bootstrap draw. It found no missing intervals, failed resamples, asymmetric mean endpoint drift, or row-pairing mismatch that would suggest a collation error. A rough width-only calculation would need K near 300,000 to reach ratio 1.02 at this N; that post-result approximation is **not** a guarantee for coverage, other cells, or a license to change K without a new prespecified plan.

**Recommendation:** Keep I6's FAIL verdict and stop before I7. For a Gaussian method, assess the already implemented exact calculation as a separate candidate, with a reviewed implementation/validation plan; if a stochastic control is still needed, predefine its K, resources, and full-cell gate before another independent run. Binary s3/s4 remain a separate report-only limitation. Do not promote these I6 values into pmed or change the gate after the result.
